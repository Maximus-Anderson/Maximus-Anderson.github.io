## Why This Bike

I saw a cafe racer that Milezero Racer built from a CB750F Clubsport and wanted one. I also wanted to work on a carbureted engine. I had learned how carburetors work in theory but had never had my hands on one, and fixing a bike that didn't run was a way to learn. The bike I got is a 1976 CB750F that hadn't run in years.

## Initial Assessment

The bike didn't run when I got it. The tires were dry rotted, so I built a center stand from scrap wood and replaced them first.

![Makeshift center stand](images/CB750_Center_Stand.jpg)
> The center stand I built from scrap wood to change the dry rotted tires.

Then I checked the four things an engine needs: spark, compression, air, and fuel.

Compression was 130 to 135 psi on all four cylinders. The SOHC forums give 150 to 180 psi as healthy, 120 as the low end, and under 100 as needing work. So the engine was even across cylinders, which is a good sign, but on the low side. The air filter was torn and I replaced it. The carburetors were the main problem. Their float pins were seized from corrosion, so cleaning alone wasn't going to fix them.

## Electrical System

The electrical system had three problems. The headlight and tail light drew too much current, turning on the high beams cut power to the whole bike, and the turn signals didn't work. The previous owner had also installed a lithium battery whose BMS couldn't supply what the bike needed, so I replaced it with an AGM battery.

I traced the harness and found loose and corroded bullet connectors all through it. I cleaned or replaced the bad ones and checked continuity afterward. Spark was weak, but the coils tested fine. The coil boots measured well above spec resistance where they meet the plugs, so I replaced the boots and the plugs.

For lighting I built a headlight from a Suzuki GS850 housing with high-output LEDs, and swapped the tail light for an LED one. Before the swap, switching the lights on dropped the voltage by nearly half a volt. After the swap the draw was negligible, the turn signals worked, and the high beams no longer cut power.

![LED tail light swap](images/CB750_LED_Tail.jpg)

## Carburetor Swap

The original carburetors were too corroded to rebuild. I found a donor bike and took its Keihin 7A carbs, which have about the same jetting as the originals. I cleaned them, set the float levels, synced all four, and installed them.

![CB750F Carburetors](images/CB750F-Carbs.jpg)
> The Keihin 7A carbs from the donor bike, cleaned and synced before they went on.

The donor carbs got the bike running, but they have been a problem since. They run rich, clog regularly, and lose their tune. I have retuned them six times and fully rebuilt them four times, resetting the float levels and cleaning the jets each time. The plugs have fouled repeatedly. Fixing the carbs is the top priority.

## Timing

With the spark fixed and the carbs on, the bike started, but it ran on three cylinders and lugged to a stop after a few minutes. I checked the static timing with the procedure from the SOHC forums. That uses a 12 V test light on the points while the engine is turned by hand, with top dead center confirmed by the timing marks and a finger over the plug hole. Timing was retarded on cylinders 2 and 3. After I adjusted it, all four cylinders fired. I skipped dynamic timing because the bike ran well after the static adjustment, so I still need to check it.

## First Miles and What Still Needs Work

The first long ride was 100 miles and the bike finished it. Several problems are left. Fuel economy is poor because the carbs run rich, and the petcock has to be on reserve to flow enough fuel. The clutch is notchy and needs a rebuild. The left front caliper seeps slowly and leaves a film on the rotor before each ride. The seep hasn't soaked the pad yet, and I don't know why.

## What's Next

Reliability comes first: the clutch rebuild, the brakes, and the carbs. After that the cafe build starts. I want to make a seat from carbon fiber nylon to cut weight and clean up the tail. The tank will get a clear coat to protect the steel and keep some of the patina. The mirrors vibrate at speed, so I plan to replace them with lower ones that damp better.
